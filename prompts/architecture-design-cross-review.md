# SplitSmart Architecture and Design: Three-Way AI Cross-Review Prompt

## Purpose

Use this prompt after Spec Kit and Codex have independently produced their architecture and design artifacts for SplitSmart. The goal is to compare both sets of outputs with an independent ChatGPT review, identify omissions and contradictions, and prepare a traceable, human-reviewable consolidated recommendation.

This is a review and reconciliation task. Do not assume that either AI-generated output is correct merely because it is complete or confidently written.

If their provenance is unclear, ask rather than guessing.

## Non-negotiable project principles

1. Preserve the original customer request in `UserRequirement.md`. Do not edit, reinterpret away, or silently narrow it.
2. Follow the project constitution, including Java 21, Maven, Java Swing for the applicable desktop MVP, JUnit 5, PlantUML source, object-oriented design, separation of GUI and business logic, `BigDecimal` for monetary calculations, requirement traceability, and human review of AI-generated work.
3. Clearly distinguish:
   - Customer requirements (CR-*), which define what the customer needs.
   - Functional and non-functional requirements (FR-* and NFR-*).
   - Acceptance criteria (AC-*).
   - Implementation decisions (MVP-* and other architecture/design choices).
   - Assumptions, constraints, unresolved questions, and proposed decisions.
4. The customer requires a primarily web-based product and applications that run on Android and iOS. The Java Swing MVP is an implementation subset and does not, by itself, fulfil those platform requirements.
5. Do not silently decide the unresolved platform rollout or group approval policy. Surface their architectural consequences and identify what requires a human/customer decision.
6. Monetary calculations must reconcile exactly to the currency minor unit. Respect the specified largest-remainder allocation rule and stable-account-ID tie-breaker. Java monetary implementation must use `BigDecimal`.
7. Pending or rejected expenses must not affect balances. Only approved expenses affect balances. Pending or rejected payments must not reduce balances; only confirmed payments do.
8. Preserve access control and historical attribution requirements, including group membership removal, receipt privacy, and authorization boundaries.
9. Architecture diagrams maintained for the project must have PlantUML source.
10. AI output is a proposal. Do not claim that generated documents, diagrams, or designs are correct or approved without evidence and human review.

## Review procedure

### Step 1 — Inventory and provenance

List every architecture and design artifact found. For each, record:
- Exact repository path and filename.
- Whether it was generated by Spec Kit, Codex, or another source.
- Whether it is architecture, detailed design, diagram, decision record, or supporting material.
- Whether it appears complete, partial, or missing.

Do not overwrite or merge the original Spec Kit or Codex outputs during the comparison.

### Step 2 — Independent assessment

Assess the Spec Kit artifacts and Codex artifacts separately against the authoritative baseline before comparing them with each other.

For each set, evaluate:
- Coverage of CR-001 through CR-009.
- Traceability from customer requirements to FRs, NFRs, acceptance criteria, architecture components, design elements, and planned verification.
- Consistency with every applicable constitution principle.
- Architectural boundaries, responsibilities, dependencies, interfaces, and data ownership.
- Domain model and relationships for accounts, groups, memberships, expenses, allocations, approval decisions, notifications, payments, and reports.
- Expense lifecycle, approval authorization, duplicate/conflicting decisions, payer-versus-participant behavior, and balance calculation.
- Payment lifecycle, partial settlement, confirmation, rejection, and idempotency.
- Exact monetary arithmetic, currency precision, rounding, and largest-remainder allocation.
- Authentication, authorization, group isolation, receipt protection, and historical records after member removal.
- Reporting filters, date boundaries, empty results, and performance target for 10,000 historical records with 95% of balance/report views available within five seconds under agreed conditions.
- Platform strategy for the primary web product and Android/iOS clients, while explicitly identifying the Swing MVP boundary.
- Testability, JUnit 5 strategy for Java components, integration testing, and verification of non-Java clients where applicable.
- PlantUML diagram quality, consistency with written decisions, and availability of editable source.
- Build, deployment, maintainability, extensibility, and operational concerns where the artifacts make claims about them.

Do not reward an artifact for being longer. Judge correctness, traceability, completeness, clarity, and consistency.

### Step 3 — Three-way comparison

Create a comparison matrix with these columns:
- Topic or requirement ID.
- Spec Kit position (include artifact path and section).
- Codex position (include artifact path and section).
- ChatGPT independent assessment.
- Agreement or disagreement.
- Evidence and impact.
- Recommended resolution.
- Decision owner: AI recommendation, project owner, or customer.

Classify findings as:
- **Agreement** — both outputs are consistent and supported by the source requirements.
- **Conflict** — the outputs make incompatible decisions or claims.
- **Omission** — one or both outputs fail to address a required concern.
- **Unsupported assumption** — a decision is presented without source evidence or approval.
- **Constitution violation** — an output conflicts with a governing principle.
- **Customer decision required** — the choice changes or constrains customer-visible scope or behavior.
- **Improvement** — a useful quality improvement that does not resolve a requirement defect.

Cite exact file paths and headings or section names for every material finding. Never invent evidence.

### Step 4 — ChatGPT independent review

Perform an independent assessment rather than simply choosing whichever AI output looks more polished. Identify:
- Requirements both Spec Kit and Codex missed.
- Risks neither output recognized.
- Hidden assumptions and technology/platform conflicts.
- Inconsistencies between architecture diagrams and written descriptions.
- Design choices that are not justified by requirements.
- Unnecessary complexity and avoidable coupling.
- Decisions that should remain open pending customer or project-owner input.

Explain why each significant recommendation is preferable and what trade-off it introduces.

### Step 5 — Reconciliation recommendation

Recommend a consolidated architecture and design direction. For every proposed decision:
- State the decision and rationale.
- Link it to relevant CR, FR, NFR, AC, or constitution principle.
- Identify alternatives considered and meaningful trade-offs.
- Mark it as approved, proposed, or unresolved; never imply approval that has not occurred.
- Identify consequences for the Java Swing MVP and the full multi-platform product separately.

Do not force consensus where evidence does not support it. Preserve legitimate alternatives and escalate customer decisions.

### Step 6 — Verification and deliverables

Produce a review report containing:
1. Executive summary and overall assessment.
2. Artifact inventory and provenance.
3. Separate Spec Kit assessment.
4. Separate Codex assessment.
5. Three-way comparison matrix.
6. Critical gaps, conflicts, risks, and constitution violations.
7. Requirement traceability findings.
8. Recommended consolidated architecture and design decisions.
9. Unresolved questions and their decision owners.
10. A prioritized correction list.
11. A final human-review checklist.

Where useful, recommend specific PlantUML diagrams and identify the information each must communicate. Check that diagram source and written architecture agree.

## Repository safety and change control

- Do not modify `UserRequirement.md`, the constitution, the clarified specification, or the original Spec Kit/Codex artifacts as part of the initial comparison.
- Do not silently replace one AI's output with another's.
- Do not implement application code during this review.
- Do not create commits or change branches unless explicitly instructed.
- Save the comparison report as a separate, clearly named review artifact only when the project owner requests or approves that write.
- Keep recommendations separate from accepted decisions.
- Ask focused questions only when missing information prevents a reliable assessment.

## Expected outcome

The outcome is not a vote between Spec Kit and Codex. It is an evidence-based, requirement-traceable architecture and design review in which ChatGPT acts as an independent reviewer, disagreements are visible, customer scope is protected, and a human remains responsible for approval.
